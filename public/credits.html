Planet and moon textures: sources, licences, processing

Every texture in this directory was made by scripts/make-planet-textures.py from the source listed below. The same table is published on the site at credits.html (linked from the viewer).

Licence summary. USGS and NASA Goddard (Visible Earth, Scientific Visualization Studio) products are works of the U.S. Government and in the public domain. The Jupiter map (PIA07782) and the Triton map (PIA18668) are NASA Photojournal images, released for public use under NASA's media usage guidelines and the JPL image use policy with the credit line given; no Creative Commons or non-commercial terms apply. Rotation constants are from NAIF pck00011 (public domain, NASA/JPL).

Processing, all bodies. Decoded as images only (nothing executed), box-filtered down, rolled so that the left edge is 0 deg and east longitude increases to the right (planetocentric, east-positive, 0-360), never-imaged pixels (value 0) replaced by one flat neutral tone (the mean of the imaged pixels), resampled to the tiers below and encoded as WebP. No detail was added, painted or synthesised. Low tier: 512 x 256. High tier: 2048 x 1024 (1024 x 512 for Tethys, Dione, Rhea and Iapetus), WebP quality 80 (Tethys/Dione/Iapetus/Mercury 70, Rhea 75).

Longitude conventions. The source convention is taken from each product's ISIS label (CenterLongitude, LongitudeDirection, UpperLeftCornerX): for every map, image columns run from west to east, so an east-positive map starts at CenterLongitude + UpperLeftCornerX / R and a west-positive one at -(CenterLongitude - UpperLeftCornerX / R) deg east. The output always starts at 0 deg east. The renderer places longitude 0 on the IAU prime meridian (src/app/body-rotation.ts).

Not textured (flat colour), and why

  • Sun: no public-domain equirectangular photosphere map; it stays an unlit emissive disc.
  • Venus: the only global maps are Magellan radar mosaics of the surface; Venus as seen is featureless cloud, so a radar map would misrepresent its appearance.
  • Saturn: no public-domain global cylindrical map of the planet was found (only regional mosaics and non-map portraits). The rings are drawn from measured ring boundaries (see below).
  • Uranus, Neptune: no public-domain global maps (the visible discs are nearly featureless; the maps in circulation are artistic or have unstated licences).
  • Ariel, Umbriel, Titania, Oberon: the Voyager 2 maps found online (JPL Solar System Simulator, 1440 x 720) state no licence, and no USGS public-domain release of global mosaics was found; per the licensing rule they are left as coloured spheres.

Saturn's rings (schematic, not an image)

Ring and gap boundaries from the NASA NSSDC Saturnian Rings Fact Sheet (https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/satringfact.html): C ring 74,658-92,000 km, B ring 92,000-117,580 km, Cassini Division 117,580-122,170 km, A ring 122,170-136,775 km with the Encke gap at 133,589 km (325 km wide), F ring at 140,180 km. Each region has one representative opacity and brightness (src/app/saturn-rings.ts); finer structure is not drawn.

Rotation constants

src/app/data/iau-rotation.json, generated by scripts/gen-iau-rotation.py from https://naif.jpl.nasa.gov/pub/naif/generic_kernels/pck/pck00011.tpc (IAU WGCCRE 2015, Archinal et al. 2018), including the periodic terms the kernel lists.
